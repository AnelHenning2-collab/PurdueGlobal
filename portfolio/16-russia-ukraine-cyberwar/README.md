## The ASCIL Symposium

On June 17, 2022, the Tech, Law and Security Program (TLSP) at American University Washington College of Law (AUWCL) created its 1st Annual Symposium on Cyber and International Law (ASCIL) with eight themes: Cyber Dimensions, Attack — Towards Greater Precision, Accountability for War Crimes, Role of Private Actors, Human Rights in Conflict, Neutrality, Spill Over, and Regional Perspectives (Colling, 2024).

---

## Ukraine's Cyber Defense

At the beginning of 2014, Russia conflicted with Ukraine because private investors Palo Alto and Microsoft disrupted the status quo. Ukraine learned cyber operations from large private investors — how to defend with firewalls to protect critical infrastructure, threat hunting, and data migration.

By 2017, Zhora (2022) reported that Ukraine was inside a 'hybrid' war with an 'IT Army' — a volunteer movement to end Russian cyber-attacks.

---

## Russian Intelligence — Evil Corp

Heine (2024) reported German intelligence spying on Russia's GRU (Main Intelligence Directorate), Unit 29155. Russia's GRU conducted cyberattacks on NATO and EU. A cybercriminal gang called **Evil Corp Hackers** collaborated with Lockbit ransomware, led by gang leader **Maksim 'Yakubet'** who acted as liaison between Evil Corp and Russian Intelligence (Newman, 2024).

## Geopolitical Implications

U.S. current cost of aid to Ukraine: **$174 billion** for military, economic, and humanitarian aid (Kaczmarek, 2024). Ukraine's loss of revenue for domestic gas raised from 124 hryvnias ($2.95) to 502 hryvnias ($11.95) per 1,000 cubic meters.

Biden gave weapons and equipment to Ukrainian partners and ensured 50 nations are ready to unite to end Russia's war against Ukraine (Blinken, 2023).
